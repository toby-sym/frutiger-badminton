# Development plan

1. **In progress — cloud court presentation:** the procedural island, full-sphere gradient sky with a generated cloud field, distant mist-faded green ridges, sun halo, cloud banks, badminton court, lighting, and net are in place. The generated URP pipeline enables 4x MSAA, HDR and soft shadows. A blue lacquer court shader adds fine grain, broad light patches and stylized window reflections; an open glass pavilion with upper galleries, fixtures and floating bubbles frames the view. Check image quality, performance, sky, horizon, contrast, glass sorting, and composition in the Windows artifact.
2. **In progress — character and shuttle presentation:** the generated shuttlecock uses vertex-colored feather vanes with a two-sided aqua Fresnel sheen, and shot type drives its axial spin. The visible shuttle body is scaled separately from the flight root so it stays readable at the close camera without covering the player. Faceless gel athletes use continuous procedural limbs, separate elbow and knee pivots, a wet moving highlight, a woven shell lattice and subtle time-driven surface ripple across the head and torso, saturated inner forms, springy squash-and-stretch, and a gently lagging head that joins the torso without a separate neck. Their outward-angled wrist rackets use rounded translucent hoops, brighter team-coloured strings, no racket-frame shadows, a team-coloured tip trail, and a longer eased swing with elastic recoil. Linux captures `45057433625`, `45058437898`, `45064651479`, and `45065866083` confirmed the lattice, direct head join, subtle surface motion, and racket silhouette and swing; Windows development builds `45058438054`, `45064641070`, and `45065852428` passed. Continue refining body proportions and gel finish against the AEROSET reference. Shuttle and dash trails use a transparent vertex-colour shader, with shuttle emission limited to flight. Contact effects are reused between hits.
3. **In progress — controller-first local rally:** gamepad movement, aiming, jump, dash, and shot mappings are implemented alongside keyboard and mouse fallback. Movement follows the camera's screen directions and settles smoothly; aim follows the last active mouse or right stick and retains its position when the stick is released. Generated character poses react to takeoff, landing, sprinting, and racket follow-through. Airborne serves use a consistent shuttle position relative to the player, and early shot presses are buffered briefly. A compact HUD keeps the serve cue, controls, dash meter, score, and run build readable. Verify mixed input, shot timing, airborne serves, landing cues, dash feedback, aim preview, and motion in the artifact.
4. **In progress — solo 3v3 and upgrades:** the player and two AI teammates face three generated opponents, with a team-colored reticle marking the assigned returner. Friendly coverage and home formation use handoff margins to reduce jitter while responding to the player. The opposing receiver remains assigned for a whole shot and returns the shuttle from its actual position before landing; the other opponents return to their starting sides. Opponents mix clears, drops, and drives toward less-covered lanes, with shot-color trails. Solo runs are first to 7 by two points (cap 9), draft an upgrade every three player points, and offer replay with progression reset. Prism Strings adds faster player shots and a shifting pearl trail; the HUD tracks all six upgrade levels, and the run-end card recaps performance and build choices. Check coverage and opponent timing, and continue balancing the run.
5. Add competitive online 3v3 after the local rally is solid.
6. Continue improving menus, feedback, presentation, and replayability; the title menu offers Solo Circuit, controls, audio/display/camera settings, and exit with keyboard, mouse, and controller navigation. Pause and results screens can return to the title menu. A closer court-level camera follows the player alongside a gentler-follow elevated overview; verify framing, aiming and control readability in both views. The score panel labels Aqua and Coral and marks the active or next server. Synthesized cues and an Escape/Start pause overlay are in place. Development CI reuses a successful feature artifact for a matching direct merge to `main` and falls back to a fresh build when the trees differ. An optional Linux capture workflow provides title and court screenshots for visual inspection; also verify the Windows artifact and gameplay in motion.

Develop the complete glass stadium with a raised canopy, full-length galleries and a rear wall around the player camera, informed by AEROSET while keeping original presentation; check both camera views and gameplay visibility. Tune AI coverage so the player has meaningful returns and rallies can end naturally; verify scoring and feel in the Windows artifact. Keep the visual direction procedural: use generated geometry, materials, and shaders without imported 3D models or image textures. Build and inspect each increment before moving on.
