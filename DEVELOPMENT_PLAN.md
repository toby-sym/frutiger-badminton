# Development plan

1. **In progress — cloud court presentation:** the procedural island, full-sphere gradient sky with a generated cloud field, distant mist-faded green ridges, sun halo, cloud banks, badminton court, lighting, and net are in place. The generated URP pipeline enables 4x MSAA, HDR and soft shadows. A blue lacquer court shader adds fine grain, broad light patches and stylized window reflections; an open glass pavilion with upper galleries, fixtures and floating bubbles frames the view. Check image quality, performance, sky, horizon, contrast, glass sorting, and composition in the Windows artifact.
2. **In progress — character and shuttle presentation:** the generated shuttlecock uses vertex-colored feather vanes with a two-sided aqua Fresnel sheen, and shot type drives its axial spin. The visible shuttle body is scaled separately from the flight root so it stays readable at the close camera without covering the player. Faceless gel athletes use continuous procedural, slightly slimmer limbs, separate elbow and knee pivots, a wet moving highlight, a woven shell lattice and subtle time-driven surface ripple across the head and torso; landing and dash motion add a short shell wobble pulse. Saturated inner forms, springy squash-and-stretch, and a slightly smaller, gently lagging head lowered into the torso without a separate neck complete the silhouette. Their outward-angled wrist rackets use rounded translucent hoops, brighter team-coloured strings, no racket-frame shadows, and a longer eased swing with elastic recoil. Contact rings now use transparent alpha blending, a smaller scale and a softer bloom so impacts read as shuttle effects instead of oversized hoops. Linux captures `45057433625`, `45058437898`, `45064651479`, and `45065866083` confirmed the lattice, direct head join, subtle surface motion, and racket silhouette and swing; Windows development builds `45058438054`, `45064641070`, and `45065852428` passed. Continue refining body proportions and gel finish against the AEROSET reference. Shuttle and dash trails use a transparent vertex-colour shader, with shuttle emission limited to flight. Contact effects are reused between hits.
3. **In progress — controller-first local rally:** gamepad movement, jump, dash and shot mappings are implemented alongside keyboard and mouse fallback. Movement follows the camera's screen directions and settles smoothly. Generated character poses react to takeoff, landing, sprinting and racket follow-through. Serves use score-based service courts and a low diagonal trajectory; early shot presses are buffered briefly. Manual mouse/right-stick targeting and its outgoing reticle have been removed. Shot type chooses flight depth and an open lane automatically, with a mild cross-court preference from the player's court position. Tune shot balance and rally pacing in the Windows artifact.
4. **In progress — local badminton match:** singles is the default; an optional 3v3 team mode uses the generated teammates and three opponents. Singles hides support players, clamps movement to the 5.18 m court width, and keeps receivers focused on the one opponent. Match setup saves the mode, AI difficulty and first-to-15/21 target. The rally winner serves next; odd-score serves come from the server's left court and even-score serves from the right, landing diagonally. Games require a two-point lead and cap at 17 or 30. AI reaction time, movement and return reach vary by difficulty. Verify service and scoring sequences, singles coverage, difficulty tuning and 3v3 behavior in the Windows artifact; continue balancing the rally.
5. Add competitive online 3v3 after the local rally is solid.
6. Continue improving menus, feedback, presentation, and replayability; the title menu offers play, match setup, controls, audio/display/camera settings, and exit with keyboard, mouse, and controller navigation. Pause and match-result screens return to the title menu. A closer court-level camera follows the player alongside a gentler-follow elevated overview; verify framing and controls in both views. The score panel marks the serving side and its service court. Synthesized cues and an Escape/Start pause overlay are in place. Development CI reuses a successful feature artifact for a matching direct merge to `main` and falls back to a fresh build when the trees differ. An optional Linux capture workflow provides title and court screenshots; also verify the Windows artifact and gameplay in motion.

Develop the complete glass stadium with a raised canopy, full-length galleries and a rear wall around the player camera, informed by AEROSET while keeping original presentation. Add small, animated jelly spectators to the galleries. Check both camera views and gameplay visibility. Tune automatic shot placement and AI coverage so the player has meaningful rallies that end naturally. Verify scoring and match flow in the Windows artifact. Keep the visual direction procedural: use generated geometry, materials, and shaders without imported 3D models or image textures. Build and inspect each increment before moving on.
